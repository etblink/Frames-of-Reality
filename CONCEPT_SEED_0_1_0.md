# Concept Seed 0.1.0

**Project:** Frames of Reality  
**Status:** pre-inception  
**Purpose of this file:** preserve the original design intuition without prematurely turning it into canon

## 1. Founding intuition

The game begins from the idea that multiple people, factions, institutions, and communities can inhabit the same world while holding radically different models of what is happening.

Those models can be:

- sincere;
- mistaken;
- incomplete;
- manipulated;
- self-serving;
- ideologically filtered;
- strategically useful;
- or substantially true.

The world itself does not change merely because characters disagree about it.

That creates a design space around the relationship between:

> **frame, evidence, belief, action, and consequence**

## 2. Desired player experience

The player should not merely choose between labeled ideological options.

The more interesting experience is to operate inside a world where:

- testimony conflicts;
- institutions have incentives;
- factions selectively reveal facts;
- some narratives contain partial truths;
- some lies are attractive;
- some truths are costly;
- certainty is often unavailable;
- and actions taken under uncertainty have real consequences.

The player may be rewarded in the short term for believing or promoting something false and punished socially for recognizing something true.

Conversely, apparent skepticism should not automatically equal wisdom. The player can also become overconfident in contrarian or conspiratorial interpretations.

## 3. Objective world state

A central candidate design principle is:

> The simulation should maintain an underlying world state that is not reducible to any faction's description of it.

Characters and systems can expose partial, noisy, delayed, biased, or deceptive views of that state.

This principle is not yet a technical architecture decision, but it is important enough to preserve.

## 4. Moral dimension

The game may contain a subtle Christian or morally serious structure.

At seed stage this means only that the project is interested in questions such as:

- truth versus deception;
- pride versus humility;
- power and responsibility;
- mercy and justice;
- loyalty and corruption;
- temptation;
- repentance;
- sacrifice;
- consequences of treating people as instruments;
- whether truth remains binding when falsehood is advantageous.

No denominational, doctrinal, narrative, or supernatural implementation is fixed.

Theology should not be smuggled in as unquestionable game canon merely because a separate theology project exists.

## 5. Satire and tone

The world may be cynical and satirical in the sense that institutions, subcultures, media, movements, corporations, bureaucracies, criminals, influencers, activists, and ordinary citizens can all be targets of observation.

Satire should not flatten everyone into identical hypocrisy.

Different characters and groups should be allowed to be:

- genuinely noble;
- genuinely corrupt;
- mixed;
- mistaken in good faith;
- manipulative;
- self-deceived;
- or unexpectedly perceptive.

## 6. Agency

The player's epistemic behavior should matter.

Candidate behaviors include:

- investigating;
- trusting;
- verifying;
- repeating rumors;
- withholding information;
- exposing information;
- manipulating others;
- clarifying disputes;
- exploiting ambiguity;
- choosing convenient narratives;
- changing one's mind.

A strong design would make these choices consequential through systems and relationships rather than only dialogue flags.

## 7. Anti-premature-canon rule

Nothing in this seed fixes:

- city or setting;
- protagonist;
- playable structure;
- faction list;
- combat system;
- economy;
- map;
- mission structure;
- supernatural content;
- multiplayer;
- camera perspective;
- art style;
- engine;
- release model.

Those should be earned through later design work.

## 8. Initial research value

Even before production, the project may be useful to Invariant Structure Research because it creates a deliberately synthetic world in which one can separate:

- world state;
- observations;
- representations;
- beliefs;
- incentives;
- actions;
- and consequences.

That relationship is a possible research bridge, not part of the game's justification for existing.

## 9. Seed principle

> Many frames can describe one world, but the world is not created by the frames.

The game should explore what follows from that without assuming the player always has privileged access to the truth.
