# Frames of Reality

**Status:** pre-inception / preservation-first

Frames of Reality is a working repository for an open-world satirical game concept centered on competing factions, subjective frames, objective reality, manipulation, clarification, and consequences for epistemic choices.

The repository name is a **working project name**, not a locked commercial title.

## Core premise

Different people and factions may interpret the same world through incompatible narratives, incentives, loyalties, and information environments.

The game should distinguish:

- what characters believe;
- what factions claim;
- what the player is shown;
- what actually happened in the world;
- and what consequences follow from acting on incomplete, distorted, or truthful representations.

The project is interested in the tension between **subjective frames and objective reality**.

## Design posture

This repository begins before the game's mechanics, setting, characters, factions, tone, or story canon are fixed.

Early ideas are exploratory source material, not binding canon.

The project should avoid turning its philosophical interests into lectures. If the concept works, those themes should emerge through play, incentives, consequences, conflicting testimony, and the player's own decisions.

## Early design interests

The initial concept may include:

- a large open-world sandbox;
- competing factions and institutions;
- satire and cynical social observation;
- manipulation versus clarification;
- unreliable or partial narratives;
- player consequences for epistemic choices;
- moral choices whose consequences are not reducible to a simple alignment meter;
- subtle Christian or morally serious structure without requiring the game to become propaganda.

None of these is frozen.

## Relationship to other projects

Frames of Reality exists in the same global reality as the rest of the project portfolio, but it is not created to validate NFC, PGH, FCP, PCR, HiVenues, EBMM, theology, or any other project.

It may later provide an unusually useful domain for studying:

- representation versus reality;
- observer-dependent information;
- competing models of the same state;
- belief updating;
- manipulation and signaling;
- incentives and agency;
- moral consequence;
- local knowledge versus global truth.

Any cross-project relationship must be established rather than presumed.
